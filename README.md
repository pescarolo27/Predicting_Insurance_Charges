# From Data to Dollars - Predicting Insurance Charges (Python)

**Background:** As a data scientist at a top health insurance company, customers' health care information can be used to obtain various health care insights. Moreover, predictive analytics can unlock patterns to forecast health care costs for customers. Such insights will help tailor services & guide customers in planning their health care expenses more effectively.

**The Data:** The primary training dataset (`insurance.csv`) contains various details concerning customers & relevant information about their health. Follow data preprocessing, models can be built using this dataset & applied to the validation dataset (`validation.csv`) to evaluate its accuracy & real-world utility by predicting costs for new customers.

This project was done in July, 2025.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Brief Summary

This project analyzed various details regarding peoples' health & their associated medical costs. It consists mainly of data cleaning & preprocessing, modeling, & prediction evaluation. In particular, linear regression was used to estimate individuals' insurance charges based on personal & health information including their age, body mass index (BMI), sex, their region of residence, whether they smoke, & how many children/dependents are on their insurance plan. The model & its predictions were then evaluated to assess how these variables contributed to insurance charges & what specific patterns were present along with their significance.


To prepare the data for thorough analyses & modeling, the two provided datasets were examined for inconsistencies & other problems. The main insurance file contained 1,338 data points, whereas the validation dataset contained 50. While the latter did not have any inconsistencies, the main dataset had several including negative values, excessive characters, inconsistent values, & missing values. Straightforward preprocessing steps were taken to eliminate such problems; however, the missing data required some particular nuance.  
Of the 1,338 data points, 131 of them had at least one missing value. Firstly, data points that had four or more missing values were removed given that such data points don't necessarily have sufficiently meaningful information to begin with. This step removed 65 data points which accounted for nearly five percent of the dataset. Next, simple imputation methods, involving measures of centrality, were applied to resolve the remaining missing data points. The cleaned version of the dataset possessed 1,273 data points.


With the cleaned data, a linear regression model was built to predict medical charges for customers. Non-numerical data was encoded & the data was scaled & standardized. The model was evaluated using various techniques & metrics. Cross validation generalized the overall predictive performance of the model across a various number of folds, specifically between two & ten. R-squared scores & root mean square errors were also computed. Across these evaluations, the model had an average r-squared score just above 71% & a root mean square error term slightly below 6,350. The former metric in particular outperforms the established r-squared goal of 65%.

Predictions were then cast & evaluated. By analyzing the feature variables against the predicted charges, relationships & other noteworthy findings were revealed & assessed as to their significance. Firstly, customers' ages & BMI's were found to have clear, strong, positive relationships with predicted insurance costs. Furthermore, people who smoke typically see somewhat more expensive insurance bills than non-smokers. When it comes to the number of dependents on customers' insurance plans, there is an overall positive, linear relationship with predicted insurance charges, but this pattern is not distinct within small ranges. For example, for customers with between zero & four children on a plan, those with three had the smallest typical predicted charges. This ambiguity continues among customers with many children (nine to thirteen). It was hypothesized that this may be due to different plans being priced according to the number of dependents.

On the other hand, customers' sex & region of residence did not appear to have a significant relationship with their predicted insurance charges. While females' typical insurance charges were typically about \$34,000 more expensive than those of males, males saw a wider range of costs. Across the four US regions, predicted costs of customers across the southeast, northwest, & southwest US were very similar; however, customers from the northeast US had predicted charges that are at least \$34,000 less than those of any other. As such, customers from the northeast region appear to have slightly smaller insurance charges, but otherwise, there is no clear pattern.

Another relevant finding that was spotted & analyzed had to do with two clusters of individuals that were present across most of the six feature variables. More specifically, ten of the 50 customers had a predicted insurance charge of at least \$380,000, whereas the other 40 customers had costs less than \$310,000. This relatively large gap suggested a potential pattern within the data; however, upon evaluating these clusters across the six variables, it was determined that this gap & the apparent clusters were more likely a result of a lack of information rather than some particular pattern amongst the 50 customers whose data built the predictions.

In short, only customers' age, BMI, smoking status, & number of children on their insurance plan were considerably indicative of how extreme their predicted insurance charges were. Further analysis could explore multivariate relationships to determine if more specific subsets of individuals are particularly related to more or less insurance charges.


### Recommendations
Customers assessing this health insurance company would be advised that their insurance charges are generally related with getting older, having a higher BMI (more overweight), having more children (specifically more than four), & smoking.  
Of the less significant factors, insurance charges are slightly less expensive for customers in the northeast US than anywhere else (in the US). When it comes to peoples' sex, females typically see slightly higher insurance charges than males (by about \$34,000), but males did see the cheapest & most expensive insurance bills overall.
